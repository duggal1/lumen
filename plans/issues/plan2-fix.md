5. AUDIO
The current audio works. Keep it as the base, then raise it to podcast and studio grade: louder, crisper, clear, and truly quiet between phrases. Ship exactly two modes: Studio Standard (default) and Studio Optimized. Build it as a Swift DSP engine. Classic DSP first, no neural net in the default path. Only if measurement shows classic DSP can't reach the targets, add a small optional ML denoise stage behind a toggle. If DSP gets there, add no ML option.
Order matters: remove noise and reverb before any gain or compression, or compression lifts the noise floor.
Standard chain: high-pass plus automatic hum notch, then dereverb (WPE or spectral late-reverb suppression, whichever is stable and artifact-free in Swift), then two-pass offline noise reduction (whole-file minimum-statistics noise estimate, Wiener or MMSE-LSA gain with decision-directed SNR, time-frequency smoothing so there's no musical-noise warble, lookahead), then keystroke and click suppression using the recorded key-event times, then a VAD-driven soft downward expander between phrases, then a look-ahead leveler with gain frozen when there's no speech, then compressor, gentle EQ and de-esser, loudness normalization, and a true-peak limiter at -1 dBTP.
The expander is soft by design. A hard gate that chops breaths or word tails is worse than faint residual noise. The goal is gaps that sound clean without artifacts.
Optimized adds: stronger dereverb, lower noise floor and deeper expansion; proximity warmth (low shelf around 120-180 Hz, mud cut around 300-400 Hz); bandwidth extension by soft saturation of 3-6 kHz only when the source lacks real high-frequency content; multiband compression plus 20-30% parallel compression; split-band de-essing so added brightness doesn't turn sibilant; a light second expander after compression; and a 10-20% blend of the Standard result if the voice sounds over-processed.
Default loudness is -14 LUFS integrated, user-adjustable, true peak at or below -1 dBTP, no clipping. Targets (measure and report honestly): speech-to-noise-floor gap of at least 40 dB Standard and 50 dB Optimized, no pumping, no sibilance worse than the input, and a clean studio clip must not get worse. Test on real recordings: echoey room with fan, typing while talking, quiet voice, far from the mic, and a clean control. Build an A/B harness (original, Standard, Optimized, loudness-matched, switchable at the same timestamp).
